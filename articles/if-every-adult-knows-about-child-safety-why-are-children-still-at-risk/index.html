Child Safety & POCSO

If Every Adult Knows About Child Safety, Why Are Children Still at Risk?

Hema R Iyer • July 30, 2026 • Child Safety

When people hear POCSO (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012) , they often think of legal procedures and court cases.

But POCSO is much more than that.

It is a reminder that every child has the right to grow up in a safe environment, free from abuse, fear, and exploitation.

The responsibility of protecting children doesn't belong only to the police or the courts. It belongs to parents, teachers, school leaders, employers, caregivers, and every adult who interacts with children.

Awareness Is the First Line of Protection

Many cases go unreported because children don't know what inappropriate behaviour looks like. Sometimes, adults fail to recognize the warning signs or hesitate to take action.

This is why awareness matters.

Teaching children about personal safety in an age-appropriate way, encouraging open conversations, and creating safe spaces where they feel comfortable speaking up can make a significant difference.

Schools and Organizations Have a Bigger Role Than Ever

Educational institutions and organizations that work with children should not view child protection as a compliance requirement.

It should be part of their culture.

Clear policies, regular awareness sessions, background verification where appropriate, reporting mechanisms, and staff training help create environments where children feel protected and heard.

Prevention Is Better Than Response

Every incident prevented is a child protected.

By investing in education, awareness, and accountability, we reduce risks before harm occurs.

Child safety should never be treated as an annual training topic. It should be an ongoing commitment shared by everyone.

Creating safer environments for children starts with informed adults who are willing to listen, act responsibly, and never ignore warning signs.

Protecting children is not just a legal obligation. It is our collective responsibility.
