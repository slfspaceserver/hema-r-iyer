POSH & Workplace Culture

Real Stories That Show Why the POSH Act Matters

Hema R Iyer • June 8, 2026

Creating Safer Workplaces & Protecting Those Who Need It Most

A workplace doesn't become unsafe because of one big incident.

More often, it starts with small behaviors that people ignore, excuse, or stay silent about.

That is exactly why the POSH Act, 2013 exists — not just as a legal requirement, but as a framework to help organizations create workplaces where people feel respected and safe.

Over the years, I have come across situations that highlight an important truth:

Harassment is not always obvious.

Sometimes it hides behind power, jokes, messages, or even silence.

When Power Changes the Meaning of Consent

A senior leader was widely respected for delivering business results.

Behind the scenes, however, there was a relationship with a junior employee. What seemed consensual on the surface became much more complicated when concerns about career impact and power imbalance emerged.

When a complaint was eventually raised, the organization struggled to respond because the accused was one of its most powerful leaders.

The lesson was clear:

When there is a significant difference in authority, consent cannot always be viewed in a simple way. Organizations need clear policies, transparency, and Internal Committees that are prepared to handle difficult situations regardless of who is involved.

When "It's Just a Joke" Happens Every Day

A woman working in a male-dominated environment became the target of constant comments.

Remarks about her appearance.

Questions about her personal life.

Jokes that everyone else laughed at.

Individually, each comment seemed small. Together, they created an environment where she felt uncomfortable coming to work.

Only after attending a POSH awareness session did she realize she wasn't being "too sensitive."

She was experiencing behaviour that was affecting her dignity and confidence.

Workplace harassment isn't always a major incident.

Sometimes it is a series of small actions repeated every day.

Harassment Doesn't Stop at the Office Door

During remote work, a manager started sending personal messages to a junior employee.

The messages slowly moved from professional conversations to late night texts and unwanted personal attention.

Because they worked remotely, the employee initially believed the situation might not fall under workplace harassment.

It did.

The investigation revealed that similar behaviour had affected others as well.

This case reminded everyone that workplace conduct applies everywhere people work — including emails, messaging platforms, and virtual meetings.

Technology changes the workplace. Respect should not.

Speaking Up Should Not Come With a Cost

An employee reported repeated unwanted advances from her manager.

The complaint was investigated and eventually upheld.

But before the process ended, she began noticing changes.

She was excluded from meetings.

Removed from projects.

Left out of important conversations.

Although her complaint was validated, the experience pushed her to leave the organization.

This highlights an important point:

Protecting employees doesn't end when a complaint is filed.

Organizations must also prevent retaliation and ensure individuals can continue working without fear of isolation or career consequences.

Building a Workplace Where People Feel Safe

The purpose of the POSH Act is not simply to investigate complaints.

Its bigger purpose is to create workplaces where issues are addressed before they become serious problems.

Organizations can strengthen their culture by:

  • Maintaining active and well-trained Internal Committees
  • Conducting practical, scenario-based awareness sessions
  • Encouraging employees to speak up without fear
  • Ensuring leaders actively support respectful workplace behaviour

The Real Goal

Compliance is important.

But culture is what people experience every day.

Employees remember how concerns were handled.

They remember whether they were heard.

They remember whether leadership acted.

The strongest workplaces are not those without problems.

They are the ones where people feel safe enough to raise them.

Because real change begins when silence is replaced with trust.
